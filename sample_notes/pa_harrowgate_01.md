# Shift with the search advisers

The duty inspector opened the shift with the standard briefing from the force's missing person procedure, reading the risk matrix out loud. Officers rolled their eyes quietly but nobody skipped a line.

The police search adviser, POLSA in the local shorthand, is a sergeant whose search training is thicker than the inspector's. In practice the inspector asks, and then does what the sergeant says.

Mountain and lowland teams are requested through a single call to the control room. The control room operator said she keeps a list of which team leaders will actually answer the phone at night.

The officers use a handheld device to record the missing person's details. One constable said it is quicker than paper; the sergeant said it loses the detail she needs, such as why the family is worried.

Every missing person is graded high, medium or low risk at first contact. Constable Reed said that grading drives everything that follows, and that nobody likes changing it once it is written.
