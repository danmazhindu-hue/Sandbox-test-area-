# Callout night, Aldermoor team base

The pager went at 21:40 and by 21:55 eleven people were in the base room. The team leader read out the callout form line by line, which Sam later called "the ritual that stops us skipping the boring bits".

Nobody starts moving until the search manager has signed the tasking sheet. A newer member began pulling on her pack and was told, kindly but firmly, to sit down until the sheet was done. It is the standard operating procedure, though nobody cited it as such.

Hari, who has been on the team for twenty years, was never formally the deputy leader but everyone turned to him when the cloud came down. "He's got the fell in his legs," someone said. His opinion on the route cut the discussion short.

Two members checked the tracker app against the paper map before leaving the car park. One said she trusts the paper more. The app showed the casualty position as a blue dot; the map reference they were given was a hundred metres off.

On the radio the casualty was described as "walking wounded, category green". Later, at the stretcher handover, the same man was reclassified as amber because of his temperature, and the ambulance crew used their own form.

At the debrief there was tea and a joke about the handover ritual. The newer members stayed quiet and listened.
